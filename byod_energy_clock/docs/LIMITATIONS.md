# What this collection can and cannot tell us

- It can describe when participating hackers reported low energy and requested support.
- It can compare subjective ratings with measured tasks and suggest useful collection questions.
- It cannot establish that caffeine, food, sleep or breaks caused an energy change.
- Convenience sampling, attrition and hourly participation changes can distort the apparent crash window.
- First-name collisions can merge different people. Manual aliases can correct known typos but cannot resolve unknown collisions reliably.
- Device latency, practice, distractions and time-of-night confounding affect reaction measures.
- Repeated records are dependent. Cluster resampling helps quantify sampling variability but cannot fix selection bias.
- Pseudonyms and timestamps can still be identifying in a small event. Group suppression is not formal differential privacy.
- Small samples and sparse categories make model coefficients and prediction intervals fragile. Baseline comparison is required before showing model predictions.
- A suggested organizer schedule reflects observed demand. It is not a causal prescription or medical advice.
- Exact collection consent text and the actual collector implementation must be documented from the supplied instruments before final submission.
